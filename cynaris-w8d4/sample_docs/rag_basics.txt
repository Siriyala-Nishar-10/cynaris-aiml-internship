Retrieval-augmented generation, usually shortened to RAG, combines a search step with a text generation step. When a question arrives, the system first retrieves passages that are likely to contain the answer, then passes those passages to a language model together with the question. Because the model answers from supplied evidence instead of memory alone, RAG reduces hallucinations and lets teams update knowledge by changing documents rather than retraining a model. Documents are cut into chunks before indexing. Chunk size matters: chunks that are too small lose context, while chunks that are too large dilute the relevant sentence with noise. A good RAG system is evaluated on both halves, checking whether retrieval found the right chunks and whether the final answer is faithful to them.
